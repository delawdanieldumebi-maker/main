# Visual Plan — Why Scientists Can't Explain This Miracle in the Americas

Companion to `why-scientists-cant-explain-this-miracle-in-the-americas.md`. Cues follow the script's section headings.

## Reference frame analysis (Messenger of God)

| Frame | What it is | Technique | Job in the edit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hand drilling rock | Cinematic close-up b-roll, shallow depth of field, warm daylight | Stock or AI-generated footage, slow push-in | Turns a spoken claim ("geologists drilled") into a literal image |
| Drill in dust | Same scene, dust haze, soft morph | AI b-roll or crossfade with particle overlay | Hides the cut and adds texture; keeps motion going |
| Crowned Virgin painting | Colonial devotional art with visible grain | Archival image, slow Ken Burns zoom toward the face | Gives the subject a "real artifact" feel |
| Satellite map, Germany in red | Dark satellite base, red country fill, white stroke, black label tag | Map motion graphic (Google Earth Studio / map-template style) | Tells the viewer where they are whenever a place is named |
| Clock and "1899" | Gold-rimmed clock on black, blurred spinning hands, serif year | Simple 2D animation | Marks a time jump; works as a chapter break |

**Overall visual language**
- **Low-key and dark.** Black backgrounds, warm highlights, vignettes. Nothing bright or saturated except one accent color per graphic.
- **Three types of footage, rotated:**
  1. Archival art and photos with Ken Burns moves
  2. Cinematic b-roll that acts out the narration literally (stock or AI)
  3. Minimal motion graphics (maps, year cards, labels)
- **Very little on-screen text.** Place labels, years and the occasional key term only. No subtitles burned in, no big titles mid-video.
- **Pacing.** A new visual every 3–6 seconds; b-roll pushes in slowly and never sits still.
- **Typography.** Labels in a clean sans-serif (white on black tag); years and dates in a classical serif.

## Our system (same grammar, separate identity)

| Element | Reference | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| Accent color | Red map fill | Antique gold `#C9A227` fill, off-white stroke |
| Label tag | Black box, white sans | Off-white box, black sans, thin gold underline |
| Time-jump card | Gold clock spinning | Candle burning down with a serif year fading in under the flame; or an hourglass for a longer gap |
| Serif for years | Classical serif | Cormorant Garamond (free, Google Fonts) |
| Sans for labels | Generic sans | Inter or Source Sans 3 |
| Grade | Warm, low-key | Warm, low-key, plus subtle film grain and a soft vignette |

**Honesty rule for reconstructions.** The reference channel shows a drill as though the test is documented. Our script says the drilling can't be sourced, so any reconstruction b-roll carries a small lower-corner tag: *"Reconstruction"* or *"According to tradition."* The same applies to the eye-reflection claim at Coromoto.

## Sourcing notes for the editor
- **Archival art:** Use public-domain colonial paintings (Wikimedia Commons, museum open-access collections). Credit them in the description.
- **Photos of the relics and shrines:** The Coromoto relic, the Las Lajas image and the bent crucifix may be copyrighted. Clear licensing, or use your own shots or AI illustrations labeled "Illustration".
- **AI b-roll:** Fine for re-enactments. Never use it in a way that could pass as a photo of the real relic, document or study.
