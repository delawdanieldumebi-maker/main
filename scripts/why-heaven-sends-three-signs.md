# Why Heaven Sends Three Signs Before the THREE DAYS OF DARKNESS

Imagine waking up on a Thursday and the morning never arrives. The clock says nine. Then noon. Outside the window there is nothing. No sunrise, no streetlight, no glow from the house next door. You flip the switch and the bulb stays dead. Your phone won't turn on. You strike a match and it dies before it touches the wick. In the whole house, one thing gives light. A single wax candle that a priest once blessed.

That is the picture two Catholic women left behind. One was a housewife in Rome. The other was a farm girl in France who carried the wounds of Christ in her hands. They never met. They lived half a century apart. And they described the same three days.

But here is what most people miss. Neither woman described a God who strikes without warning. They described a God who knocks first. Three times. Three signs, one after another, each louder than the last. And the reason He sends them is the most important part of the whole prophecy, the part almost every video skips.

There is a sign on the earth. There is a sign in the sky. And there is a sign in the sun, the one that is said to start the final count. One of those signs, many believe, has already begun. So tonight we're going to ask a different question. Not just what the signs are, but why heaven would send them at all. And by the end, you'll see that the answer changes how you should feel about every one of them.

Let's start with the housewife.

Her name was Anna Maria Taigi. She was born in Siena in 1769 and grew up poor in Rome. She married a man named Domenico, a servant in a noble household, and she gave him seven children. Domenico was not an easy man. He was loud, quick to anger, and he did not always understand his wife's long hours of prayer.

But something happened to Anna Maria in her early twenties. She began to see a light. A small, bright disk, like a little sun, that hovered just in front of her. It stayed with her for nearly fifty years. And in that light, according to the people who knew her, she saw things. The state of a stranger's soul. Events in distant countries. Things that had not happened yet.

Cardinals came to her kitchen to ask her advice. Some accounts even say members of Napoleon's own family sought her out. And her confessor, a priest named Raffaele Natali, wrote down what she told him for years.

Among the things Natali recorded was this. God would send two kinds of punishment. The first would come from the earth itself. Wars, uprisings, and great disorder among the nations. The second would come from heaven. A darkness over the whole world lasting three days and three nights. In that darkness, nothing made by human hands would give light. Only blessed candles would burn.

Anna Maria Taigi died in Rome in 1837. In 1920, Pope Benedict the Fifteenth declared her blessed. Today her body rests in a glass casket in the Basilica of San Crisogono in Trastevere, where pilgrims still come to pray beside her.

Now notice the order she gave. Earth first, then heaven. That order is the first clue to the why. And to see it clearly, we have to travel north, to a farmhouse in Brittany.

Marie-Julie Jahenny was born in 1850 to a family of poor farmers near the town of Blain in western France. She had almost no schooling. She worked the fields, she prayed, and nobody expected anything from her.

Then, in 1873, when she was twenty-three, wounds opened on her body. In her hands. In her feet. In her side. Catholics call this the stigmata, the wounds that Christ received on the cross. Every Friday, people said, she suffered His passion in her bed.

Crowds came. So did the doctors. The bishop of Nantes sent physicians to examine her, men who were looking for a trick. A French professor of medicine, Antoine Imbert-Gourbeyre, who spent his career studying hundreds of cases like hers, came to see her for himself. He went in doubting. He came out convinced, and he defended her for the rest of his life.

But the real test of Marie-Julie was not a medical one. For years, church authorities restricted her and kept her from receiving the sacraments the way she longed to. For a woman whose whole life was the Eucharist, that was a wound deeper than any on her hands. She didn't fight it. She didn't start her own movement. She didn't call the bishop a persecutor. She waited, and she obeyed. She lived in that same humble place until she died in 1941, at the age of ninety.

A person who invents visions to become famous does not usually spend her life hidden in a farmhouse, obeying in silence. That's worth keeping in mind as we hear what she said.

So we have two witnesses. Now let's look at the three signs, and at the reason behind each one.

The first sign is the longest, and it doesn't happen in the sky at all. It happens down here.

In the messages attributed to Marie-Julie, the years before the darkness are marked by disorder in nature and disorder in people. Storms out of season. Floods and droughts that ruin the harvests. Sicknesses that doctors don't recognize. Unrest in the streets. Churches emptied, and in some places attacked. Faith pushed out of public life, as if God were an embarrassment.

That is almost exactly what Anna Maria said in Rome, in fewer words. Before the punishment from heaven, the punishment from the earth.

And here's where the why begins.

Look at what the first sign actually is. It's not something God throws down from outside. It's mostly what happens when people push God out. When a society forgets Him, it doesn't become neutral. It becomes cruel, and frightened, and divided. The first sign is a mirror. Heaven holds it up and says, look at what life becomes without Me. Is this what you want?

This is the same pattern we find all through Scripture. Before the flood, Noah spent years building the ark in plain sight. Every board he nailed was a sermon. And when the time was nearly up, God told him, in seven more days, the rain will come. Seven days. Even at the very end, a countdown. One more chance.

And many people who follow these prophecies believe this is the sign we are living in right now. Wars on more than one continent at the same time. Weather records broken year after year. Churches in parts of the world burned or vandalized. Whole countries where most young people say they have no faith at all.

I want to be careful here. Every generation has had wars and storms, and every generation has thought its own troubles were the worst. The messages never give us a year. But if this first sign is a mirror, then it doesn't matter whether we're at the start of it or the end. The question it asks is the same. Have I pushed God out of my own life?

Now the second sign. And this one happens in the sky.

On December 23rd, 1881, Marie-Julie described a darkness that would come before the great darkness. A separate event. Two days, not three. She said the sky would turn a deep red and violet, and hang so low it would seem to swallow the treetops. Thunder that wouldn't stop. A strange light in the heavens that no one should go outside to stare at.

And she said something about those two days that is easy to miss. They are not the punishment. They are the last call.

Think about that. A sky that looks like the end of the world, and it's not the end. It's a warning shot. It's heaven raising its voice. Not to destroy, but to wake up the people who slept through the first sign.

This is exactly what Jesus Himself said would happen. In the Gospel of Luke, He says there will be signs in the sun and the moon and the stars, and on earth nations will be in distress. People will faint from fear. But then He adds one line that changes everything. When these things begin to take place, stand up and lift up your heads, because your redemption is drawing near.

Lift up your heads. Not hide under the bed. Not panic. Lift up your heads.

There's a story in the Bible that explains why God would send a warning like this. It's the story of Jonah. God sent Jonah to Nineveh, a violent, pagan city, with a message. Forty more days, and Nineveh will be overthrown. That's all he said. And the whole city, from the king down to the animals, put on sackcloth and fasted and prayed.

And God did not destroy Nineveh.

Jonah was furious. He had preached destruction, and destruction didn't come. And God answered him with a question. Should I not have pity on this great city?

That's the why. A warning from God is not a sentence. It's an invitation. The prophecies of the darkness are not a schedule God is eager to keep. They're the forty days of Nineveh. They're given so that, if we turn, the worst might be softened, or shortened, or for some souls, avoided altogether.

Now, before we get to the third sign, there's an objection we have to face honestly. Because if you've spent any time online looking into the three days of darkness, you've probably seen it.

There is a famous letter, shared millions of times, said to be from Padre Pio. It describes the three days in vivid detail. Keep your windows shut. Don't look outside. Light a blessed candle. Pray the rosary. It's on websites, on prayer cards, in forwarded messages from well-meaning relatives.

Here's the problem. We tried to trace that letter to Padre Pio's own writings. We couldn't. The friars and researchers who look after his work have never been able to confirm that he wrote it. So we're going to set it aside. Not because the ideas in it are wrong, but because we won't put words in a saint's mouth that we can't show he said.

And that leads to a bigger point. Over the years, people have predicted the exact date of the three days more than once. Those dates came and went. Each time, some people lost their faith, and the skeptics laughed. So let's be clear about what the Church actually says.

The Church has not ruled on the three days of darkness. The messages of Anna Maria and Marie-Julie are what the Church calls private revelation. The Catechism says private revelations don't add anything to the faith. Their role is to help us live the faith we already have, more fully, in a particular time. You are free to believe them. You are free not to. No Catholic is required to.

But even the skeptic has to admit one thing. The response these women asked for is exactly what the Church asks of us every single day. And that's not a coincidence.

So now, the third sign. And this is the one that's said to start the clock.

According to the messages attributed to Marie-Julie, the sun itself will be darkened, in a way the whole world can see. After that sign, the great darkness will not be far away. Some accounts of her messages give a count of days. Others say she named the days of the week it would fall on, a Thursday, a Friday, and a Saturday, and a season when the days are just beginning to grow longer. But she never gave a year. The day and the hour stayed with God.

And she wasn't the only one to speak of a darkened heaven. In the 1930s, a young Polish nun named Faustina Kowalska wrote down words she said she received from Jesus. Her diary is one of the best-known spiritual books in the world, and she was declared a saint in the year 2000.

In it, she writes that before Christ comes as a just Judge, He comes first as the King of Mercy. And before the day of justice, a sign will be given in the heavens. All light in the heavens will go out, and there will be great darkness over the whole earth. Then the sign of the cross will appear in the sky, and light will pour from the places where His hands and feet were pierced.

Now, to be honest, Faustina doesn't mention three days, or blessed candles. Her words are about the end of time, and we shouldn't force them into a shape they don't have. But look at the order she was given. Mercy first. Then justice. A darkness, and then, in the middle of that darkness, light pouring from the wounds of Christ.

That's the why behind the third sign. Even when the sun goes dark, the last thing God shows the world is not His anger. It's His wounds. It's the proof of how far He was willing to go to bring us back.

And here's something else. When God sent three days of darkness on Egypt in the book of Exodus, a darkness so thick it could be felt, Scripture adds a quiet detail. All the people of Israel had light where they lived. Same night, same land, and two completely different experiences of it. The difference wasn't the walls of the house. It was who lived inside.

Now let's talk about the part almost nobody mentions. The morning after.

Because both of these women, the housewife in Rome and the farm girl in Brittany, ended their prophecies with light, not darkness.

According to what Natali recorded from Anna Maria, after the darkness there would be conversions beyond counting. Whole nations that had wandered from the faith for centuries would come back. The accounts name places like Russia, and England, and China. And Marie-Julie was given a promise in a few simple words. That the reign of Christ that follows will be magnificent. A time of peace.

The darkness, in their telling, is not the end of the story. It's the night before a sunrise.

So let's put it all together.

Why does heaven send three signs before the three days of darkness?

The first sign, on the earth, is a mirror. It shows us what the world becomes without God, so we can choose differently.

The second sign, in the sky, is a last call. Like Jonah walking into Nineveh, it's a warning given so that the warning won't have to be fulfilled.

And the third sign, in the sun, is mercy's final word before justice. Even in the deepest darkness, the last thing God shows us is the light from His wounds.

Three signs. Three knocks on the door. Not because God wants to frighten His children, but because He wants every one of them inside before the storm.
