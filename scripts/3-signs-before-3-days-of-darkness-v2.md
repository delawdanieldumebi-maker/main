Because they'll know what comes next.

For two hundred years, Catholic mystics who never met each other have described the same event. Three days and three nights of total darkness over the whole earth. No electricity. No phone screens. No flame you can strike. Only a blessed candle, burning in a house that belongs to God.

## [1:38–3:44] SIGN ONE — THE EARTH CRACKS  ·  347 words

Rome. The early 1800s.

A housewife named Anna Maria Taigi is cooking dinner for a family of nine on a butler's wages. Her husband is famous for his temper. Her life is ordinary in every way but one. The people around her later testified that for decades she saw a small, bright light in front of her, like a tiny sun. And in that light, she saw things that had not happened yet.

Here's what she saw about our future. God would send two punishments. One from heaven. But before it, one from the earth. Wars. Revolutions. Evils that men would bring down on their own heads.

Notice the order. Before heaven acts, the earth breaks. And it breaks by our own hands.

And Anna Maria wasn't someone people laughed off. Cardinals climbed the stairs to her small apartment to ask her advice. People who knew her recorded that she spoke of events in families, in Rome and in the Church before they happened.

Still, that alone could be one woman's imagination. Except that in the same city, at the same time, another woman was seeing the same thing.

Her name was Elisabetta Canori Mora. In her visions, she saw the world shaken by unbelief. She saw the Church attacked from the outside and abandoned from the inside. And she saw something that should frighten us more than any war. She saw people turning away from God, not in rage, but in boredom. Indifference. Whole nations that had been Christian for a thousand years quietly closing the door.

Now hold that against the world we live in.

## [3:44–5:34] SIGN ONE COMES HOME  ·  305 words

Elisabetta didn't just see the breakdown of the family in a vision. She lived it.

She married a young lawyer named Cristoforo Mora. Within a few years, he was jealous. Then unfaithful. Then he spent the family's money on another woman until they lost almost everything. Her own relatives told her to leave him. She refused. She took in work to feed her two daughters. And she prayed for her husband every day, for years, and nothing changed.

That's the first sign at its most painful. Not a bomb. A broken home. A husband who won't pray. A child who stopped going to Mass. A family that sits at the same table and has nothing to say to each other about God.

If that's your house right now, you're not alone. And Elisabetta's story has an ending you need to hear.

She made one prediction about her own family that anyone could check. She reportedly told those close to her that after her death, her unfaithful husband would come back to God. And not only that. He would become a priest.

It sounded impossible.

Elisabetta died in 1825. Her death broke Cristoforo open. He confessed. He repented. He joined the Franciscans. And he was ordained a priest. The man who betrayed her spent the rest of his life at the altar.

The Church studied her life, and in 1994, Pope John Paul the Second declared her blessed. Anna Maria Taigi had been declared blessed back in 1920.

So these aren't anonymous internet prophecies. They're the words of two women the Church examined and raised to the altars.

## [5:34–7:52] SIGN TWO — YOUR SOUL, EXPOSED  ·  382 words

Imagine this. You're in your kitchen. Or driving. Or asleep. And in one moment, everything stops.

And you see your soul.

Not a feeling. Not a memory. You see it the way God sees it. Every sin you've confessed and every one you haven't. Every lie. Every person you hurt. Every time God called you and you said, "Not now."

In the 1960s, in the mountain village of Garabandal in Spain, four young girls said the Virgin Mary told them this would happen to every person on earth at the same moment. They called it the Warning. One of them described it as a correction of the conscience of the world. She said it would be terrifying. And that afterward, it would be very hard for anyone to say God doesn't exist.

I have to be honest with you here. Garabandal is a separate set of messages. The Church has never approved it, and it has never condemned it. But look at the pattern. Before the outer darkness, an inner light. Before the sky goes black, every soul sees itself as it really is.

And Marie-Julie Jahenny, the French mystic we'll meet in a moment, said something very close. Before the darkness, God would give the world one last time of grace. A last call. So that no one could ever say they weren't warned.

Here's why this sign raises the stakes. After the first sign, you can still look away. You can turn off the news. But after the second sign, there's no more looking away. Everyone will know. The only question left will be what you do with what you've seen.

## [7:52–10:15] SIGN THREE — THE SKY TURNS  ·  398 words

The woman who described the third sign in the most detail was a peasant from Brittany, in western France. Marie-Julie Jahenny. Born in 1850 to poor farmers. Barely schooled.

At twenty-three, wounds opened in her hands, her feet and her side. The wounds of Christ. Her bishop sent doctors to expose her. One of them, Doctor Antoine Imbert-Gourbeyre, had spent his career studying people who claimed the stigmata. He knew every trick. He watched her for years, and he left convinced.

And then came a harder test than any doctor. For a long stretch of years, she was kept away from the sacraments, because of a dispute that wasn't her doing. A woman who lived for the Eucharist, cut off from it. She didn't rebel. She didn't go looking for a following. She obeyed in silence. She lived ninety-one years in one small farmhouse, and she died there in 1941.

That's not the life of someone chasing attention.

And this is what she said would come.

A sky the color of fire. Heavy, red, and low. Thunder that doesn't stop. Lightning that strikes fear even inside closed houses. And then the sun itself losing its light.

She described the darkness with a precision that's hard to forget. Three days and three nights, falling on a Thursday, a Friday, and a Saturday. Think about those three days. Thursday, the Last Supper. Friday, the Cross. Saturday, the silence of the tomb. The world would live through the Passion of Christ.

And in those three days, nothing made by human hands would give light. Not a light bulb. Not a flashlight. Not a phone. Only candles of blessed wax.

If you think that sounds like something invented on the edge of the faith, open your Bible. Luke chapter twenty-one. Jesus Himself says there will be signs in the sun, the moon and the stars, and nations in anguish on the earth. The prophet Joel, quoted by Saint Peter at Pentecost, says the sun will be turned to darkness before the great day of the Lord.

The saints didn't invent the sign in the sky. They were pointing at Scripture.

And here is what makes the third sign different from the other two. The first sign gives you years. The second gives you a moment of truth. The third gives you nothing. Once the sky turns, there's no more time to prepare.

## [10:15–12:03] SO WHERE ARE WE?  ·  299 words

## [12:03–14:02] THE VOICES AT THE DOOR  ·  330 words

Here is what the saints said to do when the darkness falls.

Close every door. Cover every window. Light the blessed candle. Gather your family. Pray the rosary. And whatever happens, do not look outside.

Notice what's not on that list. No bunkers. No gold. No year of canned food in the basement. What the saints asked for, you could carry in one hand.

A candle of real wax, blessed by a priest. Not a battery candle. Not a scented one from the mall. If you don't have one, take it to your parish after Mass and ask. It takes a priest less than a minute. And every year on February second, the Feast of the Presentation, the day the old Catholics called Candlemas, the Church blesses candles at Mass. A crucifix. Holy water. A rosary. A blessed image of Our Lord or Our Lady on the wall.

And if you'll be alone in those days, you are not less protected. Protection doesn't depend on how many people are in a house. It depends on Who the house belongs to.

But here's the part of the instructions that stops people cold.

Marie-Julie warned that there would be sounds out there. Cries. Footsteps. Voices.

And some of those voices, she said, will sound like people you love. A son's voice. A mother's voice. Calling your name. Begging you to open the door.

You don't open it.

Think about how hard that would be. Three nights in the dark, hearing someone who sounds exactly like your child outside in the cold. Everything in you would want to run to that door. And the saints say: stay. Pray. Don't even look through the curtain.

Some people hear that and think the instructions are cruel. That God is setting a trap for the curious. Look outside, and you're struck down.

But that's not what's going on. Not even close. And the real reason goes back to the oldest story of darkness in the Bible.

## [14:02–15:39] WHY YOU MUST NEVER LOOK OUTSIDE  ·  266 words

This exact instruction has been given twice before.

The first time was the night of the first Passover. God told the Israelites to mark their doorposts with the blood of the lamb. And then He said this, in Exodus chapter twelve: "None of you shall go out of the door of his house until the morning." That night, death passed through Egypt. The houses that stayed shut, under the sign of the lamb, were passed over.

The second time was at Sodom. Angels dragged Lot and his family out of the city and gave them one command. Do not look back. Lot's wife turned around. Just to see. And she never moved again.

Do you see the pattern?

In both stories, the closed door is not a punishment. It's a test of trust. The question was never, "What's happening out there?" The question was, "Do you believe the One who told you to stay inside?"

That's what the saints were telling us. In the darkness, the walls don't protect you. The locks don't protect you. Trust does. A family praying behind a closed door, not even peeking, is a family telling God, "We don't need to see. We know You."

And there's one line in Exodus chapter ten, during the plague of darkness, that almost everyone skips. The darkness was so thick it could be felt. But all the children of Israel had light in their homes.

Same darkness outside. Different light inside.

Which brings us to the most unsettling thing Marie-Julie ever said. Because she was told that, in some homes, that light won't come.

## [15:39–17:41] THE CANDLE THAT WON'T LIGHT  ·  339 words

In the first house, the flame catches.

In the second house, it doesn't. They strike again. And again. Nothing.

According to Marie-Julie, that's exactly what will happen. In homes where God has been shut out, the blessed candles will not light.

Let that sink in. Because it means you can do everything right on the outside and still be in the dark. You can own the candle. Hang the crucifix. Have the holy water in the cupboard. And none of it is a lucky charm.

The candle was never the protection. It was the sign of something already true. A house that belonged to God before the darkness came.

So the real preparation isn't something you buy. It's something you do. And it happens in a confessional.

To be clear, the Church has never officially ruled on the Three Days of Darkness. These are private revelations, and no Catholic is required to believe them. But nothing these women asked for goes against the Gospel. Pray. Repent. Trust. Go to confession. That's the oldest teaching there is.
