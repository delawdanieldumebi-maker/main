Why didn't Jesus take him?

Nobody can read the mind of God. And the Church doesn't require you to believe this vision at all. But the record leaves us three clues.

The first is in the prayer itself. He asked to be called in the hour of his death. And whatever anyone thought that night, that hour had not come yet.

The second is what Pius took from it. By the Spectator's account, at first he thought it meant the end. But afterward, he felt so comforted that his strength began to come back. One devotional retelling puts it simply. Christ came to comfort him, not to summon him.

The third is what he did with the time. In 1956, he wrote one of the longest and most personal letters of his whole reign, on the Sacred Heart of Jesus, pierced on the cross. It never mentions the vision. But it is the same man, writing about the same wounds he had asked to be hidden in.

Put those clues together and you get something more real than a miracle story. Not a cure. Not a summons. A visit, at the worst hour of the worst year, to a man who still had work to do. And who then had to walk through that night on his own.
